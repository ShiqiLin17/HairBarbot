HairBarbot build book

The user holds a Flowbee vacuum clipper. A small motor on the Flowbee moves the comb (the guard) in and out, so the hair length changes automatically depending on where on the head the clipper is. Two orientation sensors tell it where it is. If the clipper is in the wrong spot, or the guard isn't at the right length yet, the clipper turns off immediately.

Design change

The team replaced the big "halo" robot with a handheld device. It costs about half as much, there's far less to build in 5 weeks, and it solves the real problem for people cutting at home: knowing how to give a good haircut, not holding the clipper. It still meets the brief: the guard changes length based on where on the scalp it is, and the clipper stops immediately in the wrong spot.

01 · Device

What you are building

A printed collar clamps onto the Flowbee's head. On one side it carries a small stepper with a lead screw; on the other, a guide rod. Both drive a printed comb sleeve that slides over the Flowbee hood. The distance from the blade to the sleeve's teeth is the hair length that's left (3–40 mm). An orientation sensor sits on the collar, a distance sensor looks down from the sleeve, and a trigger button sits on the handle.

Dimensions are starting targets. Measure your Flowbee first; the collar and sleeve are sized to its hood.

02 · Sensing

How it knows where it is on the head

The head is roughly a sphere, and the Flowbee is always held pointing into it. So the direction the clipper points tells you which spot on the head it's on. The clipper sensor gives that direction; the headband sensor lets you subtract any head movement. A quick calibration (hold the clipper at the top, back, left and right) lines the two up.

The math is Kabsch's method: find the rotation that best maps the measured directions onto the known ones. It also corrects for however the headband sits. If the spots disagree by more than 15°, the app asks you to redo them.

04 · Electronics

Wiring

One ESP32 runs the guard motor, reads the sensors, and switches the relays. The trigger and the E-stop each have a second contact wired straight into the clipper relay line, so the clipper can't run without the trigger held or with the E-stop pressed, even if the code freezes.

Each loop (50 times a second)

  • Read both sensors and work out the spot on the head (yaw, pitch).
  • Look up the hair length the chosen style wants there and send it to the guard motor.
  • Allow the clipper only if the trigger is held, the spot is outside the face/ear zones, and the guard is within ±1.5 mm of the right length.
  • Mark the area under the running clipper as cut, for the coverage map.

First things to change

  • Add styles from the barber interviews in pc/styles.py.
  • Measure TOF_OFFSET on the real sleeve and set it in pc/config.py.
  • If the clipper IMU isn't mounted Z-toward-scalp, change CLIP_AXIS.
  • Check the guard's steps per mm if your lead screw isn't 2 mm.
06 · Safety

Ways the clipper turns off

The brief's core rule is that the clipper stops immediately in the wrong spot. The software checks that 50 times a second, and hardware layers back it up.

Mannequins and balloons only until RASTIC signs off. For the first human tests: team members, longest guard, someone at the E-stop.

07 · Product

What the user experiences

This is the flow the app already supports. Test every step of it in your interviews.

step 1 · 30 s

Choose

Pick a style (Even 2 cm, buzz, classic, fade, long trim) and adjust five sliders. A 3D head shows the result.

step 2 · 1 min

Set up

Put on the headband. Touch the clipper to the top, back, left and right of the head to calibrate.

step 3 · ≈10 min

Cut

Hold the trigger and move over the head. The head map fills in and the app says what's left and when to slow down.

step 4

Finish

Ends automatically at 97% coverage. Trim around the ears and neckline by hand.

Questions for barbers

  • What guard numbers do you use where for your most common cuts? (These become app presets.)
  • Which direction do you move the clippers on the top, sides and back?
  • What do beginners get wrong with home clippers?
  • Would you recommend a device like this to clients between visits?

Questions for home users

  • Have you cut your own hair? What went wrong?
  • Is wearing a headband for 10 minutes OK? Would you trust the auto-guard?
  • Phone app or buttons on the device?
  • What would you pay, compared with ~$30–45 per barber visit?
